\section*{Baseline vs. Online Assignments}

\noindent

that allocates the initial inventory. Online mode then simulates incoming policies one
at a time with a target of $U_{\text{month}}=2$ online arrivals (total monthly inventory
$U_{\text{tot}}+U_{\text{month}}$); each arrival triggers a re-optimization
that preserves existing allocations and assigns only the incremental policy to an eligible
agency (e.g., respecting ZIP admissibility). In this table, the first new policy is a
high-value (Gold) policy and its ZIP is admissible for A1, so the increment goes to A1.
For the second arrival, A1 is ZIP-inadmissible and, with ZIP cell-lock active, A1/A3/A4
stay fixed by bucket so the extra Gold unit goes to A2. Subsequent arrivals keep
all online constraints active (floors, ZIP locks, and bucket bounds). The inventory size is
$U_{\text{tot}}=10$ in batch, 11 at $X^{(1)}$, and 12 at $X^{(2)}$.
The incoming bucket sequence is

\section*{Delta (Online $-$ Batch)}

\noindent
From batch to $X^{(1)}$, A1 receives one additional Gold policy.
From $X^{(1)}$ to $X^{(2)}$, A2 receives one additional Gold policy.
Row-total deltas are $\Delta^{(1)} = [1, 0, 0, 0]^\top$ and
$\Delta^{(2)} = [0, 1, 0, 0]^\top$.

\section*{What's Not in the Batch (and why)}

\noindent
Batch mode optimizes a fixed monthly inventory $U_{\text{tot}}$. Online mode then simulates
arrivals one at a time for the configured horizon; each arrival triggers a
re-optimization that preserves existing allocations. The following rules only matter
when adding a single new policy:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Per-agency floors:} protect existing allocations; no agency can drop below
its previous total.
\item \textbf{ZIP lock (for the incoming policy):} if the new policy's ZIP is inadmissible
for agency $a$, you can lock either (i) its row total (weaker) or (ii) each row cell
(stronger). In this example we use \textbf{cell lock}: all $X_{a,c}$ are frozen at previous values.
\item \textbf{Online bucket-total conservation (new):} at each online step, bucket totals are
fixed to previous totals plus exactly one unit in the incoming bucket.
\item \textbf{Headroom (per-agency buffer):} allows extra slots when adding one policy at a
time to keep feasibility.
\item \textbf{Bucket bounds still apply online:} each new policy must keep Gold/Silver/Bronze
totals within their min/max limits.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Online Bucket-Fixed Constraint}

\noindent
Let $b^{(t)}_c=\sum_a X^{(t)}_{a,c}$ be the total in bucket $c$ at online step $t$ and let
$\kappa_t$ be the incoming bucket at step $t$. The code enforces:
\[
\sum_a X^{(t)}_{a,c}
\;=\;
\sum_a X^{(t-1)}_{a,c}
\;+\;
\mathbf{1}\{c=\kappa_t\},
\qquad \forall c\in\{\text{Gold, Silver, Bronze}\}.
\]
Therefore, non-incoming buckets are fixed from $t-1$ to $t$, and only the incoming bucket
increases by one.

\noindent
For ZIP-inadmissible agencies in online mode, the example also enforces cell lock:
\[
X^{(t)}_{a,c}=X^{(t-1)}_{a,c},
\qquad \forall c,\ \forall a\ \text{inadmissible at step }t.
\]
This prevents intra-row rebalancing for locked agencies.

\section*{Constraint Audit (Summary)}

\begin{itemize}
\item Capacity: all agencies are at capacity in batch; online headroom allows incremental arrivals.
\item Global conservation: totals are 10 (batch), then 11 and 12 online.
\item Bucket-fixed trajectory: column totals follow exactly
$[5,3,2]\to[6,3,2]\to[7,3,2]$.
\item Bucket bounds: all bucket totals remain within configured min/max bounds.
\item ZIP admissibility (online): A3 and A4 are ZIP-locked at $X^{(1)}$; at $X^{(2)}$, A1 is also ZIP-locked.
\end{itemize}
